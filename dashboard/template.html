Olist Marketplace · Sep 2016 – Oct 2018

Olist doesn't have a delivery problem.
It has a promise problem.

The marketplace pads every delivery estimate by roughly twelve days — the same padding for a 160 km São Paulo order and a 2,400 km order to Pará. That's the worst of both worlds: too slow where it's safe, too tight where it isn't. Promising per order instead of per platform cuts late deliveries by 47% without lengthening the promise at all.

Why the promise is worth protecting

Satisfaction doesn't decay with delay — it holds flat right up to the promised date, then falls off a ledge. That asymmetry is the whole argument: earliness buys almost nothing, lateness costs almost everything.

Method. 99,441 orders reduced to one analytical row each. Review scores are bimodal (57% fives, 12% ones), so driver analysis models P(1-star) rather than the mean. Time series are restricted to Jan 2017 – Aug 2018; the 2016 head and Sep/Oct 2018 tail are export artifacts. Projected satisfaction under a changed promise applies the observed score-versus-gap curve and holds physical delivery time fixed — it assumes customers react to the gap against the promise, which the flat "early" buckets support. Delivery failure explains 48.8% of 1-star reviews; the other 51.2% arrived on time and the customer was unhappy anyway. The promise is the biggest single lever, not the whole story.
