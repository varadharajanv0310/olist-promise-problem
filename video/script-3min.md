# Video script — genuinely three minutes

Budgeted, not estimated: **~340 spoken words**, which lands at 2:45–3:00 at a normal presenting pace
of 110–125 wpm. The previous script was 918 words — that was the mistake.

Cues in *italics* are screen only. Everything in quotes is what you say. Nothing else.

---

*Dashboard hero, loader finished.*

"Olist is a Brazilian marketplace. Small merchants sell through the big platforms, Olist's partners
handle delivery, and every customer rates their order one to five stars.

Leadership has two years of that across nine tables — and nothing connecting them. They know
satisfaction varies. Nobody can say what drives it."

*Scroll to the stat strip.*

"I reduced all nine tables to one row per order — ninety-nine thousand rows, sixty-five columns —
then worked through the six core questions, testing each conclusion before keeping it."

*Section 01 — the cliff. Stop scrolling.*

"This is the chart everything rests on.

Satisfaction doesn't decay as delivery slips. It holds flat right up to the promised date, then it
collapses.

Fifteen days early instead of three buys you a tenth of a star. Three days late to seven costs one
and a half stars, and triples the one-star rate.

So Olist padding every estimate by twelve days isn't bad forecasting. It's insurance."

*Section 02 — the timeline.*

"And tightening it has already been tried. Over nineteen months they cut the promise from thirty-nine
days to thirteen while actual delivery improved by under four. Their late rate tripled."

*Section 03 — the frontier. Drag the slider to 95.*

"So don't shorten the promise. Don't lengthen it. Stop making one promise for the whole platform.

Olist applies the same twelve-day buffer to a hundred-and-sixty-kilometre São Paulo order and a
two-thousand-four-hundred-kilometre order to Pará. Predict each order's delivery time instead, and
promise a chosen risk level.

*Let −47% land.*

Same twenty-one-day average promise. Forty-seven percent fewer late deliveries — on twenty-five
thousand orders the model never saw."

*Section 04 — the attribution bar.*

"One caveat leadership should hear. Delivery explains forty-nine percent of one-star reviews. The
rest arrived on time. It's the biggest single lever, not the whole problem."

*Section 06 — the seller queue.*

"And when you're ready to act, this is who to call first — ranked by what each seller actually costs
you.

Full report and code are linked below."
